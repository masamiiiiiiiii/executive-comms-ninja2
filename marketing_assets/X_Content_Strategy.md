# Executive Comms Ninja: X (Twitter) Content Strategy & Guidelines

This document outlines the strategic positioning, tactical execution, and creative guidelines for the "Executive Comms Ninja" X (Twitter) account, designed to attract and engage PR professionals and executives evaluating media training solutions.

## 1. Strategy: Positioning & Target Audience
*   **Target Audience:** The primary audience is PR professionals (agencies and in-house), corporate communications teams, and executives. They are the gatekeepers who evaluate and procure media training.
*   **Goal:** Establish Executive Comms Ninja not just as a tool, but as a thought leader in modern, data-driven executive presence. We need to reach people who care about media training and have them evaluate and advocate for our application.
*   **Positioning:** Premium, analytical, objective, and disruptive to traditional media training. We replace subjective, high-cost ($10,000+), time-consuming (8-hour seminars) coaching with instant, data-driven AI reality checks.

## 2. Tactics: Execution & Algorithm Alignment
To execute the strategy and align with current X algorithm preferences (which favor high engagement, time spent on post, rich media, and niche relevance):

*   **Content Pillars:**
    *   **Educational/Analytical (The "How"):** Breakdowns of famous keynotes or press conferences (e.g., "Why did this apology fail?"). This leverages YouTube as a "free media training library."
    *   **Contrarian/Disruptive (The "Why We Existed"):** Pointing out the flaws in traditional media training (cost, time, "sterile simulations").
    *   **Feature/Benefit (The "What"):** Highlighting specific metrics the AI analyzes (pacing, filler words, eye contact, vocal energy) and how they tie back to business impact (CAC, MRR conviction).
*   **Search & Keywords:** Integrate keywords naturally to capture intent and surface in relevant X searches: `media training`, `executive presence`, `PR strategy`, `crisis communications`, `public speaking`, `keynote presentation`.
*   **Audience Segmentation (Funnel):**
    *   **Top of Funnel (TOFU):** Broadly appealing breakdowns of recent viral CEO interviews (newsjacking).
    *   **Middle of Funnel (MOFU):** Educational threads on why "presence is measurable" and the data behind conviction.
    *   **Bottom of Funnel (BOFU):** Direct comparisons of our $49 AI analysis vs. a $10,000 consultant.

## 3. Creative Guidelines: "The New Yorker" Aesthetic
The visual and copy style must stand out from typical B2B SaaS marketing. It should feel like an editorial piece—classy, elegant, and minimalist.

### Copywriting Tone
*   Punchy, confident, and slightly provocative.
*   Short sentences. High impact.
*   "Observation over intuition." "Data from dialog." "Make them feel it."

### Visual Design (Image Assets)
*   **Gold Standard Reference:** ALL illustrations must strictly match the aesthetic of `/Users/mkito/Desktop/Executive_Comms_Ninja_Assets/ECN-background.png`. This includes the exact muted olive/sage greens, beiges, minimalist line work, and elegant negative space.
*   **Color Palette:** Warm, muted, earthy, and elegant tones (e.g., beige backgrounds, sage greens, slate blues, muted golds). Avoid harsh neon or standard "tech blue".
*   **Motifs & Imagery:**
    *   **Minimalist Vector Art:** Simple, clean illustrations rather than stock photos.
    *   **Fusion of Classic & Digital:** Blend analog concepts (a physical price tag, an analog clock, a traditional podium) with digital/data overlays (line charts, waveforms, data nodes).
*   **Typography:**
    *   **Serif Fonts:** Use classic, elegant Serif fonts (like Garamond, Playfair Display, or similar editorial fonts) for primary headings and quotes to evoke the "New Yorker" magazine feel.
    *   **Hierarchy:** Keep text on images minimal. A single, powerful statement (e.g., "Redefine the cost.") is better than a paragraph.
*   **Layout:** Embrace negative (white) space. The subject should be centered or thoughtfully offset without clutter. Let the design breathe.
